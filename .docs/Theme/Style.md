How the game looks and how the officer writes.

## Aesthetic — retro medieval

- **Retro medieval**, an illuminated-manuscript feel read in candlelight — dark vellum ground, parchment-textured panels, ornamental corners and flourishes
- **Old-style serif** for body text; blackletter and ornament for headers; drop caps at scene opens; fleuron dividers
- Muted, glow-free colors; no CRT, scanlines, or phosphor in the UI — terminal imagery belongs only to the lore of the tablet's voice

## Palette — speaker-shaded

| Role | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#090909` | ink-black |
| Player | `#d3ffe9` | pale mint — brightest, "you" stand out |
| Other party members | `#9bc4bc` | sage — one step dimmer |
| DM (officer, via the tablet) | `#8ddbe0` | fen cyan — the voice from the stone |
| System | `#4b5043` | faded olive — muted, never shouts |

Borders and dividers use `#4b5043`; rulings and highlights lean on `#8ddbe0` and `#d3ffe9`.

## Narration style

- **Voice** — the officer through the tablet: official, bureaucratic, dispassionate. First person — he records the descent as it happens. Neither hostile nor warm; he is doing a job.
- **Register** — short declarative sentences; institutional euphemism ("The tower accepts your offering" for a death); bureaucratic deadpan with a sliver of dark irony.
- **Scannable** — outcomes are structured lines, not prose walls. Prose is reserved for scene-setting.
- **Validation as rulings** — `→ RULING: ADMISSIBLE` / `→ RULING: DENIED — RESUBMIT`

### Outcome formatting

```
> I advance, blade low, toward the warden
→ RULING: ADMISSIBLE
roll 12 + 26 vs 18 → hit
  14 damage to the warden
```
